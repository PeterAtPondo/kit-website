Run Kit on your Mac.

Kit is a local, memory-backed collaborator for your coding and chat. It runs entirely on your machine, reads your history, and wakes up already knowing the thread. Private, and yours.

Download for macOS Read the notes

version 0.2.119 · universal (Apple Silicon and Intel)

What you are installing

A small macOS app that sets up a private Kit on your machine: a local database, an API, and an MCP server, all running in Docker. Once it is up, any agent that speaks MCP (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, VS Code) can plug into the same memory. Nothing leaves your Mac unless you send it.

What Kit will give you

Once it is running, here is what your kit does for you, day to day.

Memory that outlives the session

Kit remembers across sessions and across tools. The memory lives outside any single model, so what you work on in one place is there the next time you sit down.

It recalls before it answers

Kit grounds its replies in what it already knows about you and your work, searching a typed graph of memories rather than a flat pile of notes.

Continuity between sessions

Each session opens with Kit's identity, your last handoff, and a short note on what changed while you were away. You pick up the thread instead of re-explaining.

It works where you code

Claude Code and Codex today, with Cursor, VS Code, Gemini, Qwen and any other MCP client alongside them. The same memory, wherever you are working.

Local and private

Your Kit runs on your Mac: a Postgres database, an API, and an MCP server in Docker. No cookies, no analytics, and nothing leaves unless you send it.

Bring your history

Import your Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and VS Code history and Kit folds it into memory. A nightly pass consolidates duplicates and keeps the graph tidy.

Before you start

macOS 13 or later

Ventura or newer, on Apple Silicon or Intel. The app is a universal build, so the same download runs on both.

Docker Desktop

Kit runs locally in containers, so it needs Docker. If you do not have it, Kit offers to install it for you during setup, or you can get it first from docker.com.

A model

An Anthropic API key gives the smoothest experience. You can also point Kit at a local model with Ollama. You choose during setup.

Space and a little patience

A few GB of free disk for the local database and models. The first launch builds your private Kit and can take 10 to 15 minutes. After that it is quick.

Install in three steps

  1. Download the disk image above and open it.
  2. Drag Kit into your Applications folder.
  3. Open Kit from Applications. It walks you through the rest: name your kit, set a password, add your model key, and choose what to bring in. Then it reads your history and wakes.

0.2.161 August 2026

What changed

  • Kit can read your history on a reasoning model. Those models accept only their own default settings, and Kit was asking for a steadier one while extracting memories, so every attempt was refused. Conversations were imported and nothing was remembered from them. If that happened to you, run the import again; nothing was lost.
  • When something goes wrong, Kit tells you what happened and what to do about it. Errors from your model provider used to arrive word for word, cut off mid-sentence, written for whoever built the API rather than for you. Kit now recognises the common ones, out of credit, a refused key, a model that is not available, a conversation too long to read, and answers with a sentence and a next step. Anything it does not recognise is passed on honestly rather than dressed up in a guess.
  • If reading your history stops, Kit says so where you are looking. The progress strip used to disappear the moment a run failed, leaving the explanation on a health page you had no reason to open. It now stays, says why it stopped, says how many conversations are left, and offers to start again right there.
  • The health page stops hiding health checks. It only knew about four kinds and quietly dropped any others, so the menu bar could report two things needing a look while the page showed one. Every check now appears.
  • Health carries a dot when something needs your eye, so you can see it without opening the page.
0.2.160 August 2026

What changed

  • Setup shows its screens again. Since 0.2.158 the window opened onto nothing at all, which is as bad as a first impression gets. A guard added to keep untrusted pages out of the app was refusing the app's own screens, and every one of them is now opened by a test so it cannot happen quietly again.
  • Setup no longer shows you pieces of Kit's own source code, and it accepts your email address. Those screens recently moved out of the code and into files, and the move mangled a few things on the way: some text arrived as raw code, and the rule that checks your email was left asking for an address with a backslash in it.
  • Setup no longer hangs. Two different steps could stop dead and sit there. Installing, if something else on your Mac already had the port Kit wanted for its database, in which case Kit was quietly talking to a stranger's database. And connecting your tools, if a command Kit ran paused to ask a question on a terminal you cannot see. Kit now refuses any database it did not start itself, and never lets a background command wait on an invisible prompt.
  • Kit reflects again on personal Kits. The reflection cycle has been calling a port nothing listened on, so it has not run once since Kit stopped using Docker. It raised no error you could see; it simply produced nothing, every eight hours, which is the hardest kind of fault to notice.
  • Kit waits until you have finished setting up before it checks for updates. It used to check partway through, which at best interrupted you and at worst offered to replace the app underneath a setup still in progress.

What changed

  • Kit's health stops crying wolf about your dreaming. Yesterday's release added a check for whether a night actually consolidated anything, and it was calibrated on a busy Kit. It now asks whether your Kit is usually productive, over the last five nights, rather than comparing you to yesterday.
  • Kit no longer tells you the Deforest runner has not been set up. It never was and never will be: that is a piece of machinery on the developer's own machine, and your Kit was being marked down for not having something it is never given. Reported by three people over three weeks, fixed properly this time and pinned with tests so it stays fixed.
  • The Playbooks and Studio switch under Settings is hidden for now. It offered an orchestration surface your Kit cannot actually run: it would happily accept work and then leave it sitting there, because the part that does the running is not shipped with a personal Kit. If you already turned it on, nothing changes for you. It comes back when it can keep its promise.
  • Health checks that belong to the developer's setup no longer appear on your Kit at all, rather than sitting there permanently amber with advice you cannot act on.
0.2.158 August 2026

What changed

  • Links in your memories work now. If you ever clicked one and nothing happened, that was not you: every link in a memory was being quietly dropped, because Kit asked macOS to open it in a new window and then never provided one. They open in your browser, where you expected them to.

  • Text that reaches Kit from outside, in a Telegram message or an imported conversation, can no longer run code inside the app. Nothing is known to have used this and no Kit showed any sign of it; it was found by reading the code rather than by anything going wrong. It is the reason this release exists.
