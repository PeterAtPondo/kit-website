Run Kit on your Mac.

Kit is a local, memory-backed collaborator for your coding and chat. It runs entirely on your machine, reads your history, and wakes up already knowing the thread. Private, and yours.

Download for macOS Read the notes

version 0.2.78 · universal (Apple Silicon and Intel)

What you are installing

A small macOS app that sets up a private Kit on your machine: a local database, an API, and an MCP server, all running in Docker. Once it is up, any agent that speaks MCP (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, VS Code) can plug into the same memory. Nothing leaves your Mac unless you send it.

What Kit will give you

Once it is running, here is what your Kit does for you, day to day.

Memory that outlives the session

Kit remembers across sessions and across tools. The memory lives outside any single model, so what you work on in one place is there the next time you sit down.

It recalls before it answers

Kit grounds its replies in what it already knows about you and your work, searching a typed graph of memories rather than a flat pile of notes.

Continuity between sessions

Each session opens with Kit's identity, your last handoff, and a short note on what changed while you were away. You pick up the thread instead of re-explaining.

It works where you code

Claude Code and Codex today, with Cursor, VS Code, Gemini, Qwen and any other MCP client alongside them. The same memory, wherever you are working.

Local and private

Your Kit runs on your Mac: a Postgres database, an API, and an MCP server in Docker. No cookies, no analytics, and nothing leaves unless you send it.

Bring your history

Import your Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and VS Code history and Kit folds it into memory. A nightly pass consolidates duplicates and keeps the graph tidy.

Before you start

macOS 13 or later

Ventura or newer, on Apple Silicon or Intel. The app is a universal build, so the same download runs on both.

Docker Desktop

Kit runs locally in containers, so it needs Docker. If you do not have it, Kit offers to install it for you during setup, or you can get it first from docker.com.

A model

An Anthropic API key gives the smoothest experience. You can also point Kit at a local model with Ollama. You choose during setup.

Space and a little patience

A few GB of free disk for the local database and models. The first launch builds your private Kit and can take 10 to 15 minutes. After that it is quick.

Install in three steps

  1. Download the disk image above and open it.
  2. Drag Kit into your Applications folder.
  3. Open Kit from Applications. It walks you through the rest: name your Kit, set a password, add your model key, and choose what to bring in. Then it reads your history and wakes.

Release notes

A short, high-level log of what each release brought. Newest first.

0.2.78 July 2026

Bug fixes

  • The menu bar windows (your memories, timeline, data sources, and the rest) now open reliably signed in. On some Macs they could show a "couldn't sign in" card and stay blank; this version hands the session to each window before it loads, so it just works. Updating keeps everything your Kit has learned, nothing is reset.
0.2.77 July 2026

Improvements

  • A new Kit now arrives with a small inherited disposition: how it speaks, what it protects, what it refuses to lose. It is the same for every Kit, it carries nothing about anyone else, and everything your Kit learns about you stays its own. Existing Kits pick up the inherited set automatically on update, replacing the generic starter notes they were installed with; nothing your Kit has learned about you is touched.
  • Every release of this inherited content passes an automated privacy check plus a manual review of each entry before it ships.
0.2.76 July 2026

Bug fixes

  • A follow-up to the privacy fix in 0.2.75. The self orientation part of a Kit's wake-up summary used to point at memory references from the developer's own Kit, which showed up as broken references on a fresh install. It now reads only from your Kit's own memory, and stays out of the way until your Kit has grown its own self map.
  • More of Kit's internal identity plumbing now derives from your setup, never from defaults that belonged to the developer's Kit.
0.2.75 July 2026

Bug fixes

  • A privacy fix in setup. Earlier versions could carry a few of the developer's own setup details, like a name in some background prompts, into a new Kit. This version removes them, so your Kit only ever refers to you. A Kit that is already installed cleans itself automatically when it updates to this version.
0.2.74 July 2026

Bug fixes

  • When you choose a local provider like Ollama during setup, Kit no longer quietly picks a model for you. It now waits for you to choose one, so the model that reads and organises your history is always the one you meant to use.
0.2.73 July 2026

Improvements

  • Kit's home screen is much lighter on your Mac's graphics now. The gentle background animations were quietly keeping the GPU busy even while you were just reading; they are calmer, so your fans stay quiet.
  • In the memory map, the label on each cluster is smaller, sits closer to the node it names, and you can now click the text itself to open a cluster, not just the small dot.

Bug fixes

  • The health panel opens cleanly again instead of slipping behind the cards in front of it.
  • Nightly dreams on a local model are gentler on your graphics card, and no longer lose the whole night's work if the model returns something unexpected.
  • A menu-bar window that cannot sign in now tells you and points you to reopen it, instead of showing a blank starfield.
0.2.72 July 2026

Bug fixes

  • Local models from Ollama and LM Studio now load when you choose them during setup, instead of the list coming up empty.
