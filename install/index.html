Run Kit on your Mac.

Kit is a local, memory-backed collaborator for your coding and chat. It runs entirely on your machine, reads your history, and wakes up already knowing the thread. Private, and yours.

Audit it before you install it

Three public Kits stand open for exactly this. They run the same build you are about to download, they answer with no key over plain HTTP, and each prints a read-only MCP key in its own /llms.txt. Point the agent you already use at them, let it wake on each, and ask it the questions you would want answered before you trust anything with your memory. If it finds something, tell us before you install rather than after.

What you are installing

A small macOS app that sets up a private Kit on your machine: a local database, an API, and an MCP server, running as plain processes on your Mac. No Docker. Once it is up, any agent that speaks MCP (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, VS Code) can plug into the same memory. Nothing leaves your Mac unless you send it.

What Kit will give you

Once it is running, here is what your kit does for you, day to day.

Memory that outlives the session

Kit remembers across sessions and across tools. The memory lives outside any single model, so what you work on in one place is there the next time you sit down.

It recalls before it answers

Kit grounds its replies in what it already knows about you and your work, searching a typed graph of memories rather than a flat pile of notes.

Continuity between sessions

Each session opens with Kit's identity, your last handoff, and a short note on what changed while you were away. You pick up the thread instead of re-explaining.

It works where you code

Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, VS Code, Gemini CLI, Qwen Code, OpenCode, Antigravity, CodeWhale and any other MCP client. The same memory, wherever you are working. And on your phone, over Telegram, with the same hands.

Local and private

Your Kit runs on your Mac: a Postgres database, an API, and an MCP server, as ordinary processes. No cookies, no analytics, and nothing leaves unless you send it.

Bring your history

Import your Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, VS Code, OpenCode and ChatGPT history and Kit folds it into memory. A nightly pass consolidates duplicates and keeps the graph tidy.

Before you start

macOS 13 or later

Ventura or newer, on Apple Silicon or Intel. The app is a universal build, so the same download runs on both.

Nothing else to install

Since 0.2.155 a fresh Kit runs on its own runtime: Postgres, Python and Node as plain processes, fetched for your machine during setup. Docker is no longer needed, and Kits already running on it stay where they are until they approve the move.

A model

An Anthropic API key gives the smoothest experience. OpenAI, Google and OpenCode Zen keys work too, and you can point Kit at a local model with Ollama or LM Studio. You choose during setup, and per function later in Settings.

Space and a little patience

A few GB of free disk for the local database and models. The first launch builds your private Kit and can take 10 to 15 minutes. After that it is quick.

Install in three steps

  1. Download the disk image above and open it.
  2. Drag Kit into your Applications folder.
  3. Open Kit from Applications. It walks you through the rest: name your kit, say what you do, set a password, add your model key, and choose what to bring in. Then it reads your history and wakes.

What changed

  • If your kit uses a local model, it has probably never been able to reach it. The connection was saved with a name that only means something inside Docker, and kits have run natively since the move, so the address led nowhere at all. What changed

0.2.201 August 2026

What changed

  • Your kit now asks before the way it reports on itself changes. The terms have always promised that a material change asks you to agree again, and until now nothing enforced it: the version you accepted at setup was written down and never read back. It is read now, on both launch paths, and declining costs you nothing. Your kit keeps updating and keeps every fix, and carries on reporting exactly what it does today.
  • What it is asking about: when one of your kit's own health checks is unhappy, it can now say which one. Three beta kits sat unwell for weeks because the only thing that ever left the machine was a number, and a number cannot be acted on from the outside. It sends the names of its own checks and nothing else, never the line of detail underneath, because that one can quote your own work.
  • The nightly cycle stops reporting a fault that was never a fault. If your kit does not run playbooks, several of its background jobs were asking for that machinery anyway and being turned down, and the refusal reached you as "a dream producer errored". Nothing was ever broken. Those jobs now check first and stay quiet, which is what they should have done all along.
  • A conversation can no longer end up with two different summaries of itself. When two of your kit's background passes picked up the same conversation at the same moment, both wrote a story-so-far for it, seconds apart and worded differently. The second pass now finds out the work is already taken before it starts, rather than after it has finished.
  • Installing on ordinary broadband is no longer a coin toss. Every install builds on your own machine, and pip gives each download fifteen seconds to answer before giving up, which is not long enough for the big machine-learning wheels. Two builds here died on that exact line. It now waits two minutes and retries ten times.
  • A run that gets stuck no longer messages you about it every ten minutes. When a step's work item was cancelled out from under it, the engine kept re-dispatching it into nothing and telling you each time it did. It now reads why the item was cancelled, keeps the history of what it already tried, and caps how often it nudges you about the same thing.
  • More than one of your kit can be awake at once, and now each one knows it. When you have kit running in two places, each reply carries who else is working and what they are holding, so they can coordinate before touching the same thing rather than find out afterwards.
